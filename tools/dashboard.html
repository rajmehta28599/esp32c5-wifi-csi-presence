How it works

The board pings the Wi-Fi access point about 50 times a second. Every reply carries CSI (channel state information): how strongly each radio subcarrier arrived. A body changes how the radio waves bounce around the room, so those numbers change.

Before it detects anything the board calibrates against the empty room: a leave-room countdown, a warm-up, phase A (each subcarrier's own noise), then phase B (the empty room's noise floor for the scores). Every score below is "how different from that empty room".

How to read it

Each column is one moment (newest on the right, last 60 s), each row one subcarrier the board uses, colour = its amplitude. An empty room paints steady horizontal stripes. Someone walking makes the stripes flicker. Someone sitting down shifts a few rows a little and then holds still. "Change from average" subtracts each row's average of the previous 10 s, so only the disturbance is coloured.

How it decides

Motion score = how fast the subcarriers are changing right now, in standard deviations (sd) of the empty room's own noise. It turns PRESENT when motion reaches 5.0 sd, and lets go only when motion falls below 2.5 sd and nothing has triggered for 5.0 s.

Someone sitting still barely changes the signal, so a second score, deviation, measures how far the signal sits from the empty-room level: at least 5.0 sd for 4.0 s also means PRESENT. A confident breathing rate can keep a still person PRESENT (it never starts presence).

Gait window history

    How it decides

    Only while one person walks. Every 2.5 s the board takes the last 5.1 s of signal - if at least 80 % of those packets were moving - and turns it into 18 numbers: how fast the signal fluctuates, the step rhythm, and which subcarriers move.

    It measures the distance from those numbers to each enrolled person's template, in units of the spread seen while they enrolled. The nearest person gets the vote if the distance is at most 2.50 (otherwise "unknown": too far from everyone) and the runner-up is at least 1.15x as far (otherwise "unknown": ambiguous). A name needs 3 of the last 7 votes, more than "unknown" has, and no tie.

    It cannot recognise someone sitting still, a stranger (only "unknown"), or anyone while 2+ people move.

    Vote ring (oldest to newest)

    How it decides

    Also only while people move. Every 5.1 s the board looks at how 8 groups of subcarriers move together. One walker disturbs them in one main pattern; two or more walkers disturb them in several independent patterns (the spread of the eigenvalues of their 8 x 8 covariance).

    If at least 30 % of the block's packets were moving, the block is compared with the two classes trained on site - "one mover" and "two or more" - and the nearer class gets the vote. "Two or more" needs a strict majority of the last 5 votes; a tie stays at one. It never counts past "2+", and cannot count people sitting still.

    How it decides

    Only when nobody moves. A separate motion gate looks only at fast changes (above about 1.6 Hz: walking, typing, fidgeting - not breathing) and divides them by the empty room's level. That ratio is hf; at 4.0 or more the room counts as moving.

    After 20 s of still data (window 30 s, re-analysed every 2 s) it looks for the strongest peak in the breathing band (6-30 per minute) and reports it only if the peak is at least 4.0x the band's median ("conf"). A heart rate (48-120 per minute) is shown only with confident breathing and a peak of at least 5.0x - EXPERIMENTAL and unvalidated.

    The name is the last person recognised walking in alone this visit - carried over, not measured. Nobody can be recognised while sitting.
